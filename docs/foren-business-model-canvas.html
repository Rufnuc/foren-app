Business Model Canvas

A private, tamper-evident evidence vault for survivors — and the model that keeps it free for them.

Working draft 20 Jul 2026 Local-first · Zero-knowledge

Key Partners

  • Anti-violence NGOs — shelters, refuges, DV helplines who reach survivors and recommend the tool
  • Legal-aid & family law — clinics, solicitors, and prosecution-support services that use the evidence
  • Digital-security & human-rights groups — for audits, threat review, and safe distribution
  • Timestamping infrastructure — Solana and Bitcoin / OpenTimestamps for public anchoring
  • Grant-makers & foundations — gender-justice, digital-safety, and access-to-justice funders
  • Academic & forensic partners — to align with evidentiary standards

Key Activities

  • Security & product engineering; independent audits
  • Safety design — the game disguise, duress passcode, and panic-wipe
  • Blockchain anchoring & offline verification tooling
  • Trauma-informed UX and safety research
  • Partnership development & grant writing

Key Resources

  • The crypto stack — on-device AES-256 encryption + Merkle/blockchain anchoring
  • The disguise & duress system (deniability)
  • On-device OCR & speech-to-text models
  • Trust and brand within survivor & advocate networks
  • Team: security, trauma-informed product, DV & legal domain expertise

Value Propositions

Evidence a survivor can gather safely, keep private, and later prove was never altered.

  • Hidden in plain sight — opens as an ordinary puzzle game; a duress passcode shows an empty decoy, so it's safe on a shared or monitored phone.
  • Zero-knowledge & local-first — everything is encrypted on the device. No account, no cloud custody; nothing leaves unless the user exports it.
  • Tamper-evident proof — each item is hashed and anchored to public blockchains, creating an independently verifiable timestamp that holds up later.
  • Makes raw material usable — on-device OCR and transcription turn photos and recordings into searchable, organized case files, fully offline.
  • Court-ready exports — structured bundles with proofs that advocates and lawyers can verify offline.

Customer Relationships

  • Anonymous & self-serve by design — no account builds trust through privacy
  • Support routed through advocates & orgs, not identifiable direct contact
  • In-app safety guidance and help
  • Training, onboarding & documentation for partner organizations

Channels

  • Web app + disguised app-store listing
  • Trusted intermediaries — caseworkers, helplines, legal-aid clinics
  • Advocate & practitioner networks; conferences
  • Word of mouth — never ad-retargeting of survivors

Customer Segments

  • Primary — survivors documenting domestic abuse, coercive control, stalking, and gender-based violence
  • Paying — organizations women's shelters, DV helplines, legal-aid clinics, family-law firms, advocacy groups
  • Adjacent at-risk users human-rights defenders, journalists' sources, and others needing deniable, tamper-evident records

Cost Structure

  • Engineering & security salaries — the largest line
  • Independent security audits & penetration testing
  • Infrastructure — anchoring relay, batched chain fees, hosting
  • Legal & compliance — evidentiary standards, data protection
  • Partnership & training delivery
  • Grant management & operations

Revenue Streams

Organization-sponsored

The org decides who needs it

  • NGOs, shelters, legal-aid clinics & law firms buy licenses (per-seat / per-org, tiered)
  • They enrol the survivors they serve — who pay nothing
  • Predictable, budget-backed recurring revenue

Direct subscription

For anyone an org doesn't reach

  • Individuals subscribe on their own — no referral or org needed
  • Affordable monthly / annual tier; core safety features never paywalled
  • Grant-funded hardship access for those who can't pay
  • Underpinned by grants & philanthropy (DV, digital-safety, access-to-justice), partner training contracts, and later public-sector pilots

Model  Two front doors, no one gated out. Organizations sponsor the survivors they serve, who pay nothing; anyone the org network doesn't reach can subscribe directly, with grant-funded access for those who can't afford it.

Note  Revenue is the working assumption to validate; every other block reflects what Foren is and does today. Blockchain anchoring and OCR/ASR run on-device or via a self-hosted relay.
